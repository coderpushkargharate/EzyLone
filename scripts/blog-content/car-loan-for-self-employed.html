Shop owners, contractors, doctors, chartered accountants, transporters and traders buy cars on loans every day, but the process looks a little different from a salaried employee's. There are no salary slips, income can vary month to month, and lenders look much more closely at bank statements and tax returns. This guide explains how a car loan for self-employed applicants works and how to present your income so the lender sees its true strength.

Two kinds of self-employed applicants

  • Self-employed professionals (SEP): doctors, CAs, architects, lawyers, consultants and others with a professional qualification.
  • Self-employed non-professionals (SENP): traders, shop owners, manufacturers, contractors, transporters and other business owners.

Many lenders have separate rules for each group. Professionals with a registered practice are often assessed a little more easily because their income tends to be steadier.

How lenders work out your income

For salaried people, the lender takes net salary. For the self-employed, the lender has to decide what your real, repeatable income is. The common approaches are:

  1. ITR-based income. The income shown in your last one or two income tax returns, sometimes averaged, with depreciation and certain items added back.
  2. Banking-based assessment. The lender looks at your average bank balance or regular credits over 6 to 12 months. Useful when ITR income understates the business, but terms can differ.
  3. Turnover or gross receipts. Some programmes for professionals or GST-registered businesses use turnover as a starting point.

Once the lender has a monthly income figure, it applies the same FOIR rule as for salaried applicants. Our guide on car loan eligibility explains FOIR with an example.

Common eligibility rules

  • Business running for a minimum period, often 2 to 3 years
  • ITRs filed for at least the last 1 to 2 years
  • A minimum annual income, set by each lender
  • A healthy credit score and no recent defaults
  • A stable business address, owned or rented

Documents you will usually need

  • PAN, Aadhaar and current address proof
  • ITRs for the last 2 years with computation of income
  • Profit and loss account and balance sheet, audited if applicable
  • Business bank statements for 6 to 12 months, plus personal account if income comes there
  • Business proof: GST registration, Udyam certificate, trade licence or shop and establishment registration
  • For professionals: degree and practice registration
  • Car quotation from the dealer

The ITR problem and how to handle it

Many small business owners show a modest income in their ITR to reduce tax. The trouble is that lenders base eligibility on that same figure. If your ITR says ₹4 lakh a year, it is hard to qualify for a large EMI even if the business earns more.

What helps:

  • File ITRs on time, every year, and report income honestly. A rising income trend helps your case.
  • Route business receipts through your bank account. Large cash deposits without explanation weaken a file.
  • Keep business and personal accounts clear, so the lender can see what the business earns.
  • Ask about banking-based programmes if your ITR income is low but bank credits are strong.
  • Add a co-applicant with income, such as a spouse or a business partner. See co-applicants and guarantors explained.

Planning the EMI around uneven income

Business income rarely arrives on the same date every month. To avoid bounced EMIs:

  • Choose an EMI date a few days after your usual strongest inflows
  • Keep a buffer of at least two or three EMIs in the account
  • Pick a tenure that keeps the EMI comfortable in your weakest month, not your best
  • Use the EMI calculator to test different amounts

A single bounced EMI can hurt your credit score and attract charges, so a small buffer is worth it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a car loan with only one year's ITR?

Some lenders accept one year, often with stronger banking or a co-applicant. Two years of ITRs give you more options.

Is the interest rate higher for self-employed borrowers?

Not necessarily. A strong credit score, documented income and a good down payment can get you terms similar to salaried applicants. Read what decides your car loan interest rate.

Do GST returns help?

Yes. GST returns show turnover and business continuity, and many lenders ask for them.

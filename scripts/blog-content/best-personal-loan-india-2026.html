Two personal loan offers can look almost the same in an advertisement and still cost you very different amounts. The difference usually hides in the processing fee, the insurance added to the loan, the prepayment rules, or simply the tenure. The "best" personal loan isn't the one with the lowest headline rate. It's the one with the lowest total cost that fits your budget.

This guide shows you how to compare personal loan offers in 2026 the way a lender would: using the annual percentage rate (APR), the total cost and the fine print.

Step 1: Compare the APR, not just the interest rate

The interest rate tells you what you pay on the borrowed amount. The annual percentage rate (APR) also includes fees such as the processing fee, so it reflects the real yearly cost. RBI requires lenders to give borrowers a Key Fact Statement (KFS) before the loan agreement is signed. It shows the APR, all charges and the repayment schedule in a standard format, which makes it the best document for comparing offers.

A worked example

You need ₹3,00,000 for 3 years and receive two offers (both are only examples):

Offer AOffer B
Interest rate13%14%
Processing fee3% + GST = ₹10,6200.5% + GST = ₹1,770
Monthly EMI₹10,108₹10,253
Total interest₹63,895₹69,118
Total cost (interest + fee)₹74,515₹70,888
Approximate APR (fee deducted upfront)about 15.5%about 14.4%

Offer A has the lower interest rate and EMI, but Offer B is about ₹3,600 cheaper overall because of its much smaller fee. Also, with Offer A, ₹10,620 is deducted from the amount you receive, so you get less money in hand.

Step 2: Check the total cost for the same tenure

Lenders sometimes quote a longer tenure to make the EMI look smaller. Always compare offers for the same amount and the same tenure. Use the EMI calculator to put every offer on the same footing.

Step 3: Read the fine print

What to checkWhy it matters
Fixed or floating rateMost personal loans are fixed; with a floating rate, your EMI or tenure can change
Prepayment and foreclosure chargesIf you expect a bonus or other income, you may want to close the loan early
Lock-in periodSome loans can't be closed in the first few months
Part-payment rulesWhether you can pay off part of the loan, how often, and at what cost
Insurance added to the loanCredit life cover may be useful, but it adds to the cost; ask whether it's optional
Late payment and bounce chargesThese add up quickly if a mandate fails
Disbursal amountIs the fee deducted from the loan, or paid separately?

Step 4: Check who is lending

  • Make sure the lender is a bank or an RBI-registered NBFC, or that the app clearly names the regulated lender behind it.
  • The loan should be credited from the lender to your own bank account.
  • Never pay an "advance fee" to anyone to "release" a loan. Read our guide on how genuine loan facilitators work and how to spot fake agents.

Step 5: Think about service, not just price

  • How quickly does the lender respond, and is there a local branch or contact?
  • Can you see your loan statement and outstanding balance online?
  • How easy is it to get a foreclosure letter and NOC at the end?

How to get better offers in the first place

  1. Improve your credit score before applying. Pay all EMIs and card dues on time, and keep card usage low. See how to improve your CIBIL score.
  2. Reduce existing EMIs if possible. Lenders look at how much of your income is already committed.
  3. Don't apply everywhere at once. Multiple hard enquiries in a short time can lower your score and make lenders cautious.
  4. Keep documents consistent. Name, date of birth and address should match across PAN, Aadhaar and bank records.
  5. Consider a co-applicant with a good income and credit score, if the lender allows it.
  6. Consider a secured option. If you have a running car loan, a car loan top-up may be cheaper than an unsecured loan.

Quick comparison checklist

  • Same amount and tenure for every offer
  • APR from the Key Fact Statement
  • Total cost = total interest + all fees
  • Amount actually credited to your account
  • Prepayment, part-payment and lock-in rules
  • Optional add-ons removed if you don't want them
  • Regulated lender, and money credited directly by the lender

Frequently asked questions

Is the lowest interest rate always the best personal loan?

No. A lower rate with a high processing fee or expensive add-ons can cost more overall. Compare the APR and the total cost.

Where do I find the APR?

In the Key Fact Statement that the lender gives you before you sign the loan agreement. Ask for it if you don't receive it.

Does applying with several lenders hurt my score?

Each formal application can add a hard enquiry. A few, spread sensibly, are normal; many in a short time can lower your score. Shortlist lenders that suit your profile first.

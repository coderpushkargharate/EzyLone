Your car can do more than get you from home to work. If you own a car outright, or have repaid a good part of its loan, you can raise money against it through car refinance, also called a loan against car. Because the car is the security, these loans are often easier to get and cheaper than an unsecured loan.

This guide explains how used car refinance works, how lenders value your car, who is eligible, what it costs and the risks you should understand before you pledge your vehicle.

Refinance, loan against car and balance transfer: what's the difference?

OptionYour situationWhat happens
Loan against car (refinance)You own the car with no loan, or the loan is closedThe lender gives you a fresh loan and records hypothecation on the RC
Refinance of a running loanYou still have a car loanThe new lender closes your existing loan and gives you extra funds on top, based on the car's value
Balance transferYou still have a car loan, and want a better rateThe new lender closes your existing loan; usually no extra money (see our balance transfer guide)

How the car is valued

The loan amount is based on the car's current market value, not what you paid for it. Lenders usually arrange an inspection or valuation and look at:

  • Make, model and variant and how well that model holds its resale value
  • Age of the car (year of manufacture and registration)
  • Kilometres driven and physical condition
  • Ownership history: first-owner cars are usually valued higher
  • Insurance and claim history, and whether the RC is clean (no pending challans or disputes)

The lender then lends a percentage of that valuation, and the percentage varies by lender, car age and your profile. Most lenders also cap the car's age at the end of the loan, so an older car may only qualify for a shorter tenure.

Who is eligible?

  • The car must be registered in your name (or your firm's, for business vehicles).
  • A steady income that can support the EMI: salaried, self-employed or business owners.
  • A reasonable credit history. Refinance is often easier than an unsecured loan for people with a thin credit file, but serious recent defaults still matter.
  • If there is a running loan, a good EMI record on it.

Documents checklist

  • PAN card, Aadhaar and address proof
  • Original RC, and a valid insurance policy
  • Income proof: salary slips and bank statements, or ITRs and bank statements for self-employed applicants
  • If there is a running loan: its account statement and foreclosure letter; if a previous loan was closed: its NOC and proof that the hypothecation was removed
  • Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate, if asked

What it costs

As an illustration, a loan of ₹4,00,000 for 48 months at 13% (an example rate) works out to an EMI of about ₹10,731 and total interest of about ₹1,15,088. Rates on loans against used cars are usually higher than on new car loans, because the security is older and loses value faster. On top of interest, expect:

  • A processing fee charged by the lender
  • Valuation or inspection charges, if any
  • RTO charges for adding the hypothecation to your RC
  • Foreclosure charges on your old loan, if you are refinancing a running loan

Compare offers using the annual percentage rate (APR) in the lender's Key Fact Statement, which includes these fees. Try different amounts and tenures on the EMI calculator.

Smart uses, and risky ones

Sensible: funding working capital for your business, consolidating expensive credit card debt into one lower-rate loan, or a medical or education expense where you have a clear repayment plan.

Risky: using the loan for spending you could avoid, or borrowing the maximum amount on a car you depend on for your livelihood. If you miss payments, the lender can repossess the car after following due process, and you lose both the car and your credit score.

Step-by-step process

  1. Share your car details and needs with the lender or a facilitator.
  2. The car is inspected and valued.
  3. You submit KYC, income and vehicle documents.
  4. The lender checks your credit report and issues a sanction letter with the amount, rate, tenure and fees.
  5. You sign the agreement; hypothecation is recorded on the RC.
  6. The loan is disbursed to your bank account (after closing any existing loan).

Frequently asked questions

Can I refinance a car that is still on loan?

Yes. The new lender closes your existing loan and pays you the remaining eligible amount, if any, after the closure.

How old can the car be?

Each lender sets its own limit, usually based on the car's age at the end of the loan. Newer cars get larger amounts and longer tenures.

Can I keep using the car during the loan?

Yes. You keep driving the car; the lender's hypothecation is only recorded on the RC until the loan is repaid.

Is refinance available for commercial vehicles?

"How much car loan can I get on my salary?" is the first question most buyers ask, and it is the right one. The answer decides which car you can comfortably afford, how much down payment you need and how long the loan should run. This guide shows how banks and NBFCs work out car loan eligibility, with a simple worked example you can redo with your own numbers.

The two limits every lender applies

A lender looks at two separate ceilings and gives you the lower of the two:

  1. What the car can support. Lenders fund a share of the car's price (often up to 80 to 90 percent of the ex-showroom or on-road price for a new car, and less for a used car). The rest is your down payment.
  2. What your income can support. Even if the car qualifies for a large loan, the EMI must fit within the lender's income rules.

Most rejections and reduced offers come from the second limit, so that is where to focus.

FOIR: the rule behind income-based eligibility

Lenders use the Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR): the share of your net monthly income that can go towards all EMIs put together, including the new car loan. The exact limit differs by lender, income level and profile, but it commonly falls somewhere between 40 and 60 percent of net income.

The calculation is straightforward:

  • Net monthly income × FOIR limit = maximum total EMI
  • Maximum total EMI − EMIs you already pay = room for the car loan EMI
  • That EMI, at the offered rate and tenure, converts into a loan amount

A worked example

Say your take-home salary is ₹50,000 a month, you already pay a ₹5,000 EMI on a personal loan, and the lender uses a 50 percent FOIR (an example figure).

  • Maximum total EMI: ₹50,000 × 50% = ₹25,000
  • Room for the car EMI: ₹25,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹20,000

What else changes your eligibility

Credit score and repayment history

A strong credit score and a clean record of on-time payments can get you a better rate and a higher FOIR allowance. Late payments, settled accounts or many recent loan enquiries pull eligibility down. If your score needs work, read our guide on improving your CIBIL score before a loan.

Type of employment

Salaried applicants with a stable employer usually have the simplest assessment. Self-employed applicants are assessed on ITRs, business turnover and banking, and the process looks a little different. We cover that in car loans for the self-employed.

Existing EMIs and credit card dues

Every running EMI reduces the room for a new one. Some lenders also count a share of your credit card outstanding as an obligation. Closing a small loan before applying can noticeably increase your car loan eligibility.

Co-applicant income

Adding a spouse or parent with income as a co-applicant lets the lender consider combined income. See how co-applicants and guarantors affect eligibility.

Age, job stability and city

Lenders typically set minimum and maximum ages, minimum work experience and minimum income levels. These rules vary between lenders, which is why comparing more than one is useful.

How to increase the loan you are eligible for

  • Pay a bigger down payment so you need to borrow less.
  • Close or reduce small loans and clear credit card dues before you apply.
  • Add an earning co-applicant.
  • Choose a slightly longer tenure, keeping in mind the extra interest.
  • Declare all regular income that you can document, such as rental income backed by bank credits.
  • Avoid applying with many lenders at once, which adds hard enquiries to your credit report.

How much car should you actually buy?

Eligibility is the maximum a lender will allow, not what is comfortable. A practical guide is to keep the car EMI well below your FOIR ceiling, so that fuel, insurance, servicing and savings still fit. Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum salary for a car loan?

It depends on the lender and the car. Many lenders publish a minimum net monthly income for salaried applicants. If your income is close to the minimum, a larger down payment or a co-applicant helps.

Does checking my eligibility affect my credit score?

Working out eligibility with a calculator does not. A formal application where the lender pulls your credit report is a hard enquiry, so apply only once you have chosen a lender.

Can I get 100 percent on-road funding?

Some lenders offer high funding for certain profiles and models, but it is not guaranteed. Higher funding usually means a higher EMI and more interest, so a down payment is still sensible.

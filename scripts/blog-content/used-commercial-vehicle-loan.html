For many transport operators, a good used truck, tipper or bus is the smartest first buy. It costs far less than a new vehicle, starts earning immediately and keeps the EMI within reach. A used commercial vehicle loan makes that possible, but lenders look closely at both you and the vehicle. This guide explains how lenders value a used CV, the eligibility rules, documents and the checks you should do before you pay the seller.

Why buy a used commercial vehicle?

  • Lower price and therefore a smaller loan and EMI
  • Faster start: the vehicle is available immediately, often with permits
  • Slower depreciation than a new vehicle in its first years
  • A stepping stone for first-time buyers to build a repayment record

The trade-off is higher maintenance, a shorter remaining life and usually a higher interest rate than a new CV loan.

How lenders value a used CV

The lender's approved valuer inspects the vehicle and estimates its current market value, based on:

  • Make, model and body type
  • Year of manufacture and age at the end of the loan
  • Kilometres run, engine and chassis condition, tyres
  • Accident history and repairs
  • Demand for that model in your region

The loan is then a share of this valuation, not of the price you agreed with the seller. Many lenders also set a maximum vehicle age at the end of the loan tenure, which limits how long you can borrow for an older vehicle.

A worked example

Say a used truck is valued at ₹16 lakh and the lender funds 75 percent (an illustrative figure). The loan would be ₹12 lakh, and you pay ₹4 lakh plus transfer and insurance costs. At an illustrative 13 percent for 48 months, the EMI is about ₹32,193.

Before you agree, estimate the truck's monthly earnings after fuel, driver salary, tolls, maintenance and insurance. The EMI should fit comfortably within what is left, with a buffer for slow months. Use the EMI calculator to compare tenures.

Eligibility

Existing operators

Fleet owners and operators with a repayment record on earlier vehicle loans have the most options. Lenders look at the number of vehicles you own, your repayment history and your transport income.

First-time buyers

Drivers turning owners and first-time buyers can get loans too, but lenders may ask for a larger down payment, a guarantor, proof of a transport contract or driving experience. Our commercial vehicle loan guide covers first-time buyers in detail.

Credit score

Documents you will usually need

About you

  • PAN, Aadhaar and address proof
  • Bank statements for 6 to 12 months
  • ITRs, if available
  • Proof of existing vehicles owned and their loan statements
  • Driving licence, especially for owner-drivers
  • Transport contracts or work orders, if you have them

About the vehicle

  • Copy of the RC
  • Valid fitness certificate, permit, insurance, PUC and road tax receipts
  • If the seller has a running loan: the lender's NOC and Form 35
  • Transfer forms (Form 29 and Form 30)
  • Valuation report from the lender's valuer

Checks to do before paying the seller

  • Match the engine and chassis numbers on the vehicle with the RC
  • Check vehicle details on the official Parivahan portal, including registration, fitness validity and any pending challans
  • Confirm the hypothecation status: if a lender's name is on the RC, the old loan must be closed and the NOC obtained
  • Check permits and their validity, and whether they can be transferred
  • Get a trusted mechanic to inspect the engine, gearbox, suspension, chassis and tyres
  • Check insurance claim history, which can reveal past accidents
  • Pay through the bank, not cash, and keep a written sale agreement

The same principles apply to used cars; see our used car loan guide for a similar checklist.

Costs beyond the EMI

  • Processing and documentation fees
  • Valuation charges
  • RC transfer and hypothecation endorsement fees at the RTO
  • Insurance renewal or transfer
  • Immediate repairs and new tyres
  • Permit transfer fees, where applicable

Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum age of a used truck for a loan?

It varies by lender and vehicle type. Many lenders set a limit on the vehicle's age at the end of the loan. Ask before you finalise the vehicle.

Can I refinance a commercial vehicle I already own?

Many lenders offer loans against a commercial vehicle you own, which can raise working capital. Terms depend on the vehicle's value and your record.

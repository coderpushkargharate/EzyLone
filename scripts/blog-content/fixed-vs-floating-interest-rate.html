When you take a loan, one of the most important choices is often the least understood: should the interest rate be fixed or floating? The answer changes how much you pay, how predictable your EMI is and even whether you can prepay without charges. This guide explains fixed vs floating interest rates in plain language, with an example of what a rate change does to your EMI.

What is a fixed interest rate?

The rate is set at the start and stays the same for the full tenure, or for a stated fixed period. Your EMI does not change because of market movements.

Common for: car loans, two-wheeler loans, most personal loans and many commercial vehicle loans.

What is a floating interest rate?

The rate is linked to a benchmark and moves when the benchmark moves. For banks, many retail floating-rate loans are linked to an external benchmark such as the RBI repo rate, plus a spread. NBFCs and some older bank loans may use an internal benchmark. When the benchmark rises, your rate rises; when it falls, your rate falls.

Common for: home loans and loans against property.

What a rate change does to your EMI

Take a ₹20 lakh loan against property for 15 years, starting at an illustrative 9.5 percent floating rate. The EMI is about ₹20,884.

If the rate rises by 1 percent to 10.5 percent, the EMI for the same remaining tenure would be about ₹22,108, around ₹1,200 more every month. Many lenders keep the EMI the same and extend the tenure instead, which means you pay for longer and more interest overall.

Check what your lender does when rates change. RBI has asked lenders to be transparent about this and to give borrowers options, such as increasing the EMI, extending the tenure within limits, or prepaying. Track your loan on the lender's app and use the EMI calculator to see the impact.

Watch out for these terms

  • "Fixed" for a few years only: some loans are fixed for an initial period and then become floating. Read the agreement.
  • Reset frequency: how often the floating rate is revised, such as every three months.
  • Spread: the margin over the benchmark. It can change only under conditions stated in your agreement.
  • Switching fee: some lenders allow a switch between fixed and floating, or a lower spread, for a fee.
  • Flat rate quotes: a "flat" rate is not the same as a reducing-balance fixed rate. Ask for the APR. See what decides your car loan rate.

Which should you choose?

Fixed may suit you if:

  • You want certainty, with no surprises in your monthly budget
  • The loan is short, such as a 3 to 5 year car or personal loan
  • You think rates are more likely to rise than fall

Floating may suit you if:

  • The loan is long, such as a 10 to 15 year loan against property, where rates will likely move both ways
  • You plan to prepay and want to avoid prepayment charges
  • You have room in your budget for a higher EMI if rates rise

Nobody can predict interest rates reliably. Choose the option whose worst case you can live with.

Prepayment: a hidden advantage of floating

RBI rules bar banks and NBFCs from charging foreclosure or prepayment penalties on floating-rate term loans taken by individuals for purposes other than business. Fixed-rate loans can carry charges, which matters if you expect a bonus or plan to close early. Read more in our guide to prepayment and foreclosure.

Frequently asked questions

Are car loans fixed or floating?

Most car loans in India are fixed-rate. A few lenders offer floating options.

Can I switch my floating loan to a lower rate?

You can ask your lender to move you to its current lower spread, often for a fee, or move the loan to another lender through a balance transfer. Compare the total cost first.

Does the lender have to tell me when my rate changes?

Lenders are expected to communicate rate changes and their effect on EMI or tenure. Check your statements and the lender's app regularly.

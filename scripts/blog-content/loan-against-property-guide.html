If you own a house, flat, shop or commercial building, it can help you raise a large amount at a lower interest rate than most unsecured loans, without selling it. A Loan Against Property (LAP) is commonly used to expand a business, fund higher education, pay for a wedding or a major medical need, or consolidate expensive debts.

Because your property is the security, LAP also carries more risk than other loans. This guide explains how it works, how much you can get, the eligibility rules, the legal and technical checks, the costs, and how to borrow safely.

How a loan against property works

You mortgage a property you own to a bank or NBFC. You continue to live in it, run your business from it or rent it out; the lender simply holds a charge on it until the loan is repaid. When you repay in full, the lender returns your original documents and releases the charge.

LAP can be taken against:

  • Residential property: a self-occupied or rented house or flat
  • Commercial property: a shop, office or commercial building
  • Industrial property, with some lenders

Lenders usually prefer properties with clear titles and approved plans in established areas. Agricultural land and some types of plots are often not accepted.

How much can you borrow?

The loan is based on two limits, and you get the lower of the two:

  1. Loan-to-value (LTV): lenders lend only a percentage of the property's market value, decided by their own valuer. Residential property usually gets a higher LTV than commercial property.
  2. Repayment capacity: your income, after existing EMIs, must support the new EMI.

Tenure and EMI: an example

LAP tenures are much longer than personal loan tenures, often running up to 15 years and sometimes longer. Here is an illustrative example for ₹20,00,000 at 11% per year (an example rate):

Eligibility

  • Salaried or self-employed individuals, and businesses (proprietorships, partnerships, companies)
  • Age: usually from 21 or 25 years, with the loan ending before the lender's upper age limit
  • Income: a stable, documented income (ITRs are especially important for self-employed applicants)
  • Ownership: the property must be in your name or a co-applicant's; all co-owners usually have to join the loan
  • Credit history: a clean repayment record

The legal and technical checks

This is where LAP takes longer than other loans. Lenders typically do:

  • Legal verification: a lawyer checks the title chain (who owned the property before you), the sale deed and other ownership documents, and whether there are disputes or existing mortgages.
  • Technical valuation: an engineer or valuer visits the property, confirms its location, construction and approvals, and estimates its market value.
  • Encumbrance check: confirming the property isn't already mortgaged or under any legal charge.

What it costs

  • Processing fee, charged by the lender
  • Legal and technical valuation charges
  • Stamp duty and registration for the mortgage, as per state rules
  • Prepayment or foreclosure charges, if applicable. RBI does not allow prepayment charges on many floating-rate loans taken by individual borrowers, but rules differ by loan type and purpose. Check what applies to your loan in the Key Fact Statement and agreement.

Borrow safely

  • Borrow for a clear purpose with a repayment plan, not for routine spending.
  • Don't stretch the EMI. If you miss payments, the lender can take legal action under the SARFAESI Act to recover its dues from the property.
  • Understand fixed vs floating rates. Most LAPs are floating; your EMI or tenure can change when rates change.
  • Keep copies of every document you hand over, and get a written list of the originals the lender keeps.
  • After closing the loan, collect all original documents and a release letter, and make sure any registered charge is satisfied.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a loan against a property that already has a home loan?

Some lenders offer a top-up on a running home loan, or a balance transfer with a top-up. A fresh LAP from another lender is usually not possible while the property is already mortgaged.

Can I use LAP for my business?

Yes. LAP is commonly used for business expansion and working capital. Tell the lender the purpose; some products are designed specifically for business owners.

How long does a loan against property take?

Longer than a personal loan, because of the legal and technical checks. Having clean, complete property documents ready makes the biggest difference.

When you need money quickly, the credit card in your wallet is the easiest option. But "easy" and "cheap" are not the same. This guide compares a personal loan vs credit card borrowing, including revolving credit, card EMI conversion and cash withdrawals, with a worked example so you can choose the cheaper route.

Three ways to borrow on a credit card

1. Revolving the balance

If you pay only the minimum amount due, the rest of the bill carries over and attracts interest, usually at a high monthly rate. You also lose the interest-free period on new purchases until the balance is cleared. This is the most expensive way to borrow on a card.

2. Converting a purchase or the bill into EMIs

Most card issuers let you convert large purchases or your outstanding bill into fixed EMIs at a stated rate, often with a processing fee. This is cheaper than revolving, but compare it carefully with a personal loan.

3. Cash withdrawal

Withdrawing cash on a credit card usually attracts a cash advance fee plus interest from the day of withdrawal, with no interest-free period. Avoid this except in a true emergency.

How a personal loan differs

A personal loan gives you a lump sum in your bank account, repaid in fixed EMIs over a fixed tenure, often one to five years. The rate is fixed at the start, based on your credit score, income and the lender. There is a processing fee, and the full cost appears in the Key Fact Statement.

When a credit card makes sense

  • You can repay the full amount within the interest-free period
  • A small, short-term need of a few weeks
  • A genuine no-cost EMI offer where the full cost is truly nil (check for processing fees and lost discounts)

When a personal loan is better

  • You need a larger amount for several months or years
  • You are already carrying a card balance month after month
  • You want a fixed EMI and a clear end date
  • You want to consolidate several card balances; see our guide to debt consolidation loans

The hidden effect on your credit score

Credit bureaus look at how much of your available card limit you use. Running a card close to its limit for months can lower your score, even if you pay the minimum on time. Moving that balance to a personal loan and keeping the card balance low can help your credit utilisation, as long as you do not run the card up again.

Smart habits whichever you choose

  • Never pay only the minimum amount due if you can avoid it
  • Set up auto-pay for at least the minimum, so you never miss a due date
  • Read the card's most important terms and conditions for rates and fees
  • For a loan, read the Key Fact Statement and check the APR
  • Use the EMI calculator to compare monthly payments

What lenders and card issuers look at

Your credit card limit was set when the card was issued, based on your profile at that time. A personal loan is assessed fresh: the lender checks your current income, existing EMIs and credit report. If your income has grown and you have a good repayment record, you may qualify for a larger personal loan at a better rate than your card can offer. Frequently asked questions

Is credit card EMI conversion cheaper than a personal loan?

Sometimes, for short tenures and promotional offers. Compare the rate, the processing fee and GST, and the total amount you will pay.

Can I use a personal loan to pay off my credit card?

Yes, and if the loan rate is much lower it can save money. Close the loop by keeping card use low afterwards.

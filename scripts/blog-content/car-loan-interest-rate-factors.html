Two people can buy the same car, from the same dealer, on the same day, and be offered different car loan interest rates. That is not unfair; it is how lenders price risk. Knowing what decides your car loan interest rate helps you improve the offer you get and compare offers properly.

Why rates differ from person to person

Banks and NBFCs start from a base or benchmark rate and add a margin based on how risky the loan looks. Anything that makes the loan look safer can bring the rate down, and anything that makes it look riskier can push it up.

The factors that matter most

1. Your credit score and history

This is usually the biggest factor. A high score with a long record of on-time payments signals low risk. Missed EMIs, written-off or settled accounts and high credit card use lead to higher rates or rejection. See our guide on improving your CIBIL score.

2. Income and employer

A stable, documented income and a well-known employer reduce perceived risk. Lenders often have preferred employer categories for salaried applicants. Self-employed applicants are judged on the strength and consistency of their business income.

3. Down payment and loan-to-value

The more you pay upfront, the less the lender is exposed if the car's value falls. A bigger down payment can help you get a better rate, and it always reduces the total interest you pay.

4. New or used car

Used car loans usually cost more than new car loans because the car's value is harder to judge and falls further. The older the car, the higher the rate tends to be. Read more in our used car loan guide.

5. Car model and segment

Popular models with strong resale value are easier to finance. Some lenders run model-specific schemes with manufacturers or dealers.

6. Tenure

Some lenders price longer tenures slightly higher. Even if the rate is the same, a longer tenure means more interest overall.

7. Your relationship with the lender

Existing customers with a salary account, savings or a past loan repaid well may be offered better terms.

8. Fixed or floating rate

Most car loans in India are fixed-rate, but some lenders offer floating-rate options. Read fixed vs floating interest rates to understand the difference.

Look beyond the headline rate

  • Flat vs reducing rate. A "flat" rate is charged on the original amount for the whole tenure and looks lower than an equivalent reducing-balance rate. Always ask for the reducing rate or the APR.
  • Processing and documentation fees. These add to your real cost.
  • Bundled insurance or extended warranty. Optional add-ons should not be forced on you.
  • Prepayment and foreclosure charges. See our guide to car loan prepayment and foreclosure.
  • The Key Fact Statement (KFS). It shows the annual percentage rate (APR), which includes fees, so you can compare offers fairly. Our article on comparing loan offers with APR explains how.

How to get a better car loan rate

  • Check your credit report a few months before you buy, and fix errors
  • Pay down credit card balances and avoid new loans before applying
  • Increase the down payment if you can
  • Compare more than one lender, including your own bank
  • Ask about festive or model-specific schemes, then read the fine print
  • Keep the tenure as short as your budget allows

Already paying a high rate on a running loan? A car loan balance transfer may help, once you subtract the switching costs.

Frequently asked questions

Is the dealer's finance offer always the best?

Not always. Dealer-linked offers can be convenient and sometimes competitive, but compare the APR and fees with other lenders before you decide.

Can I negotiate my car loan rate?

Sometimes. A strong credit profile, a larger down payment and a competing offer give you room to ask for better terms, especially on fees.

Does a zero-percent offer mean no cost?

Often the cost is built into the car price, a higher processing fee or a lost cash discount. Compare the total amount you pay.

Your credit score is the first thing most lenders look at. It affects whether your loan is approved, how much you can borrow and what interest rate you pay. The good news is that a score isn't fixed. With the right habits, most people can improve it within a few months, and fixing errors in your report can help even faster.

This guide explains what a CIBIL score is, what affects it, how to check your report for free, how to fix mistakes, and a practical plan to improve your score before you apply for a car loan, personal loan or loan against property.

What is a CIBIL score?

A credit score is a three-digit number, usually between 300 and 900, that summarises how you have handled credit in the past. "CIBIL score" is the popular name, after TransUnion CIBIL, but India has four credit bureaus (credit information companies): TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax and CRIF High Mark. Each keeps its own report on you, so your score can differ slightly between them.

As a broad guide, many lenders treat scores above about 750 as good. But each lender sets its own cut-offs and also looks at your income, existing EMIs and the type of loan.

Step 1: Get your credit report, free

RBI rules entitle you to one free full credit report every year from each credit bureau. You can request it on each bureau's website. Checking your own report is a "soft enquiry" and does not lower your score.

Step 2: Check the report for mistakes

Errors are more common than you might expect. Look for:

  • Loans or cards you never took (a possible sign of identity misuse)
  • Closed loans still showing as active
  • Payments marked late that you made on time
  • Wrong personal details: name spelling, date of birth, PAN or address
  • Duplicate accounts

If you find an error, raise a dispute with the credit bureau online, and also write to the lender that reported it. RBI rules require bureaus and lenders to resolve disputes within a fixed time. Keep copies of NOCs and closure letters for every loan you repay; they are your proof.

Step 3: Fix the habits that move the score

Pay every EMI and card bill on time

This is the biggest factor. Set up auto-debit for at least the minimum amount due on cards, and keep enough balance in your account on EMI dates. Even one bounced EMI shows up in your report.

Keep credit card usage low

Try to keep your card balance well below your limit. A common rule of thumb is to stay under about 30% of the limit. Paying the bill before the statement date lowers the balance that gets reported.

Clear overdue amounts properly

If you have an overdue account, talk to the lender and pay it off in full where you can. A "settled" status, where the lender accepts less than you owe, is treated negatively for years. Ask for a "closed" status and an NOC after full payment.

Stop applying for new credit for a while

Every formal loan or card application adds a hard enquiry. Several in a few months tell lenders you may be in financial stress. Pause new applications for a few months before a big loan.

Don't close your oldest card

An old card with a good record helps your credit age. If it has no annual fee, keep it and use it lightly.

Be careful about guaranteeing loans

If you are a guarantor or co-applicant on someone else's loan, their missed payments can affect your report too.

How long does it take?

Lenders report your repayments to the credit bureaus regularly, so good behaviour starts showing within a few reporting cycles. Corrected errors can reflect as soon as the dispute is resolved. Recovering from serious defaults takes longer, often a year or more of clean repayments, but every on-time payment helps.

A 90-day plan before you apply for a loan

  1. Week 1: download your reports from the bureaus and list every error.
  2. Weeks 1–2: raise disputes for errors; collect NOCs for closed loans.
  3. Every month: pay all EMIs and card bills in full and on time, keep card usage low.
  4. Throughout: make no new loan or card applications.
  5. Day 90: check your report again, then apply to one or two lenders that suit your profile.

Already have a low score and need a loan?

  • A secured loan, such as a car loan top-up or loan against your car, may be easier to get than an unsecured personal loan.
  • A co-applicant with a good score and income can strengthen the application.
  • Borrow a smaller amount that you can repay comfortably, and use it to rebuild your record.
  • Avoid anyone who promises approval "regardless of CIBIL" for an upfront fee. That is a common fraud. Read how to spot fake loan agents.

Frequently asked questions

Does checking my own CIBIL score reduce it?

No. Checking your own score or report is a soft enquiry and has no effect. Only lenders' checks on your loan or card applications count as hard enquiries.

Can someone remove negative entries for a fee?

No one can legally remove accurate information from your credit report. Only errors can be corrected, through the bureau's dispute process, which is free.

I have no credit history. Is that a problem?

Some lenders are cautious with first-time borrowers. A small secured card or a small loan, repaid on time, helps you build a history.

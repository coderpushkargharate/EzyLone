Growing a business takes capital: a new machine, a second shop, more stock before the festive season, or simply working capital to bridge slow payments. Unsecured business loans are often limited in size and costly. A loan against property for business lets you unlock the value of a home, shop or commercial building you already own, usually at a lower rate and over a longer tenure. This guide explains how it works, how much you can get and how to use it safely.

How a business LAP works

You mortgage a residential or commercial property to a bank or NBFC. The lender values the property, checks its legal title and assesses your business income. You receive a lump sum, or in some cases an overdraft limit, and repay it in EMIs over a period that can run to 10 or 15 years, depending on the lender and your age.

The property stays in your name and you keep using it. The lender holds a charge on it until the loan is repaid.

Common business uses

  • Expanding to a new location or renovating premises
  • Buying machinery, equipment or a commercial vehicle
  • Working capital to fund stock and receivables
  • Repaying expensive short-term business debt
  • Buying out a partner or funding a new product line

How much can you borrow?

Two limits apply, and you get the lower one:

  1. Loan-to-value (LTV): lenders usually lend a share of the property's market value, often around 50 to 70 percent, with residential property sometimes getting a higher share than commercial property. Each lender sets its own limits.
  2. Repayment capacity: your business income must support the EMI along with existing obligations.

For example, a ₹30 lakh business LAP over 10 years at an illustrative 10.5 percent has an EMI of about ₹40,480. The lender will want to see that your business cash flows can comfortably cover that, with room to spare. Test different amounts on the EMI calculator.

Eligibility and documents

Business documents

  • ITRs for the last 2 to 3 years with computation of income
  • Audited profit and loss account and balance sheet
  • Business bank statements for 12 months
  • GST registration and returns
  • Business registration: Udyam, partnership deed, or company incorporation documents
  • KYC of the applicant, partners or directors

Property documents

  • Title documents: registered sale deed and the chain of earlier deeds
  • Encumbrance certificate
  • Land records, mutation and tax receipts
  • Approved building plan and occupancy or completion certificate, where applicable

Term loan or overdraft?

Term loanOverdraft against property
How you get moneyFull amount at onceA limit you draw from as needed
InterestOn the full outstandingOnly on the amount used
Best forOne-time needs: expansion, equipmentFluctuating working capital
RepaymentFixed EMIsInterest monthly; limit may reduce over time

Not every lender offers both. If your need is mainly seasonal stock, ask about an overdraft facility.

Costs to check

  • Interest rate and whether it is fixed or floating; see fixed vs floating rates
  • Processing fee
  • Legal and technical valuation charges
  • Stamp duty and registration on mortgage documents, where applicable
  • Prepayment and foreclosure charges. Loans for business purposes may carry charges even at floating rates, so read the agreement carefully.
  • Insurance linked to the loan, which should be optional unless clearly required

Using a business LAP safely

  • Borrow for productive uses that will generate income to repay the loan.
  • Stress-test the EMI: could you still pay if sales dropped for a few months?
  • Keep a cash buffer of a few EMIs in the business account.
  • Do not over-borrow just because the property supports a larger amount.
  • Think carefully before mortgaging the family home for a risky venture.

Business LAP vs unsecured business loan

Unsecured business loans are faster and do not put property at risk, but amounts are smaller and rates higher. A business LAP suits larger, longer-term needs where you are confident of steady cash flows. For a broader comparison, read loan against property vs personal loan.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a LAP on a commercial shop?

Yes. Many lenders accept commercial property, sometimes with a lower LTV than residential property.

Can a new business get a LAP?

It is harder. Lenders usually want 2 to 3 years of business history and ITRs. A co-applicant with steady income can help.

How long does a LAP take?

Because of legal and technical checks, LAPs generally take longer than personal loans. Complete documents speed things up.

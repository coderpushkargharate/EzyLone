For transporters, a truck, bus, tipper or pick-up isn't just a vehicle; it's the business. A commercial vehicle loan lets you add to your fleet or buy your first vehicle without tying up all your working capital. But lenders look at commercial vehicle loans differently from car loans: they care about the vehicle's earning potential, your experience in transport and the route or contract it will run on.

This guide explains how commercial vehicle loans work, who is eligible (including first-time buyers), the documents you need, how to plan the EMI against your vehicle's income, and what to check before you sign.

Which vehicles can be financed?

  • Goods carriers: light commercial vehicles (LCVs), pick-ups, small trucks, intermediate and heavy commercial vehicles (ICV and HCV), trailers
  • Passenger vehicles: buses, school buses, staff buses, taxis and tourist vehicles
  • Construction and mining: tippers and similar equipment
  • Three-wheelers for goods or passengers

Both new and used commercial vehicles can be financed. Used vehicles are valued by the lender, and the vehicle's age at the end of the loan is limited.

Who is eligible?

Lenders usually group applicants into categories:

Applicant typeWhat lenders look for
Fleet ownerNumber of vehicles owned, repayment track on existing vehicle loans, business financials
Small operator (1–2 vehicles)Experience in transport, earnings from the existing vehicle, bank statements
First-time buyerDriving or transport experience, a contract or assured work, a guarantor, and often a higher down payment
Captive user (vehicle for own business)The business's financials and how the vehicle supports it

A valid commercial driving licence (for owner-drivers), a clean credit history and stable income are common requirements across lenders.

How much can you borrow?

Lenders fund a share of the vehicle's price (or valuation, for a used vehicle). The share depends on your category, track record, the vehicle type and the expected earnings. Body building costs for trucks may or may not be included, so ask the lender.

Planning the EMI against vehicle income

The most important question isn't "how much will the bank give?" but "can the vehicle comfortably pay its own EMI?". As an illustration, a loan of ₹15,00,000 for 48 months at 13% (an example rate) means an EMI of about ₹40,241.

Work out your vehicle's realistic monthly earnings and subtract:

  • Diesel and lubricants
  • Driver's salary and allowances
  • Tolls, permits and taxes
  • Insurance, maintenance and tyres
  • Days without loads, and the vehicle's downtime for repairs

What remains should cover the EMI with a buffer to spare. Freight income is seasonal in many sectors. In Odisha, for example, mining, construction and agricultural transport have their busy and slow periods, so plan for the lean months too. Some lenders offer repayment structures matched to seasonal cash flows; ask about them.

Documents checklist

  • KYC: PAN card, Aadhaar and address proof (for a firm or company, its PAN, registration documents and partners' or directors' KYC)
  • Bank statements, usually for the last 6 to 12 months
  • ITRs and financials, if available
  • Proof of existing vehicles (RCs) and their loan repayment track, for existing operators
  • Driving licence, for owner-drivers
  • Transport contract or work order, if you have one; very helpful for first-time buyers
  • Proforma invoice from the dealer, or the RC and valuation for a used vehicle

Costs and terms to check

  • Interest rate, fixed or floating, and the annual percentage rate (APR) in the Key Fact Statement
  • Processing and documentation fees
  • Prepayment or foreclosure charges
  • Insurance: commercial vehicles need the right cover, including third-party liability
  • Penalty for late EMIs, and how bounced payments are handled

Tax note

When a commercial vehicle is used for business, the interest on the loan and the vehicle's depreciation are generally treated as business expenses. The rules depend on your business structure, so speak to your chartered accountant.

Frequently asked questions

Can a first-time buyer get a commercial vehicle loan?

Yes, though lenders are more careful. Driving or transport experience, a confirmed contract, a guarantor and a higher down payment all improve your chances.

Can I refinance my existing truck or bus?

Yes. Many lenders offer loans against existing commercial vehicles and top-ups on running loans, based on the vehicle's value and your repayment record. Read more about how vehicle refinance works.

What tenure is common for commercial vehicle loans?

Tenures commonly run for several years, depending on the vehicle type and whether it is new or used. A shorter tenure means a higher EMI but less interest.
